DEPARTMENT OF FISH
NSE 7 ENTERPRISE FIREWALL 7.6 ADMINISTRATOR
STORY-BASED STUDY PLAN

PURPOSE

This plan prepares Samwise, an experienced Fortinet administrator, for the Fortinet NSE 7 Enterprise Firewall 7.6 Administrator exam.

The exam expects applied knowledge of:
- System configuration
- Central management
- Security profiles
- OSPF and BGP
- IKEv2 IPsec
- ADVPN

The story is not decoration placed around a textbook. Each episode introduces a realistic operational need, incident, project, audit, or design decision that gives the technology a reason to exist.

SESSION DESIGN

- Target duration: 20–30 minutes.
- Use an adaptive balanced approach: brief direct teaching combined with one question at a time.
- Begin with a short story scene and a concrete engineering problem.
- Diagnose prior knowledge before teaching.
- Compress familiar material quickly.
- Spend more time on weak mental models, behaviour prediction, troubleshooting, and exam distinctions.
- Include optional mini-labs where useful.
- Include short troubleshooting moments throughout and dedicated incident episodes.
- End every episode by generating:
  1. session-summary-ep-XX.txt
  2. knowledge-register-ep-XX.txt

SEASON STRUCTURE

SEASON 1 — FOUNDATIONS
Theme: Samwise inherits a small but growing government network.
Agency stage: One office floor attached to the Tuggeranong data centre, with Gungahlin as the secondary data centre.

EP-01 — THE FLOOR ABOVE THE SERVERS
Topics:
- Enterprise firewall solution
- DCFW, ISFW, NGFW, and DEFW roles
- Secure network use cases
Story:
Samwise is promoted from senior administrator into an architecture role. Señor Gonzalez asks him to explain why the Department cannot simply place one large firewall at the internet edge.
Learning outcomes:
- Match firewall roles to business and traffic requirements.
- Explain north-south versus east-west inspection.
- Distinguish firewall model from firewall role.
Exam lens:
- Explain secure-network use cases using Fortinet solutions.
Optional mini-lab:
- Label the initial Department topology by firewall role.

EP-02 — THE FIRST PACKET
Topics:
- Life of a packet
- First packet versus established-session packets
- Policy lookup, routing, NAT, inspection, session creation
Story:
A fisheries licensing application works for existing users but fails for new connections after a policy change.
Learning outcomes:
- Narrate initial packet processing at useful exam depth.
- Explain why later packets normally follow the session table.
- Identify where routing, policy lookup, NAT, and UTM inspection occur.
Troubleshooting focus:
- Debug flow versus session inspection.
Mental model:
- First packet visits the front desk; later packets use the issued access card.

EP-03 — TOO MANY NETS IN THE NET
Topics:
- VLANs
- VLAN interfaces
- Zones
- Inter-VLAN policy
Story:
Research, licensing, finance, and guest devices all share the same broadcast domain. An audit requires segmentation without rebuilding the floor.
Learning outcomes:
- Explain why VLANs create Layer 2 segmentation but do not independently enforce security.
- Design zones and policies around Department functions.
- Predict traffic behaviour between VLANs.
Optional mini-lab:
- Create a four-VLAN design for the Tuggeranong floor.

EP-04 — ONE BOX, SEVERAL DEPARTMENTS
Topics:
- VDOM purpose and operation modes
- Inter-VDOM links
- Management and resource separation
Story:
A protected marine intelligence team needs administrative and routing separation while remaining on shared hardware.
Learning outcomes:
- Explain when VDOMs are appropriate instead of only VLANs.
- Distinguish administrative separation, routing separation, and policy separation.
- Predict traffic flow through inter-VDOM links.
Season checkpoint:
- Samwise presents the initial architecture to Junior.
- Short review and knowledge-register calibration.

SEASON 2 — RESILIENCE

Agency stage: Tuggers and Gunners become active operational data centres connected by fibre.

EP-05 — THE SILENT HEARTBEAT

Learning outcomes:

- Predict split-brain risks and failover behaviour.

EP-06 — WHO WEARS THE CROWN?
Topics:

- Priority, override, uptime, monitored interfaces
- Primary selection and re-election
Story:
After the Tuggers unit returns from maintenance, executives expect it to become primary immediately.
Learning outcomes:

- Explain the effect of override.
- Identify common exam distractors involving priority and uptime.

EP-07 — TWO CLUSTERS, ONE SERVICE
Topics:
- Active-passive versus active-active
- FGCP versus FGSP
- HA design across data centres
Story:
An application owner asks for both data centres to process traffic simultaneously and assumes one stretched cluster will solve everything.
Learning outcomes:
- Compare FGCP and FGSP.
- Explain when state sharing is useful and what is not synchronised.
- Choose a design based on failure domain and traffic symmetry.
Troubleshooting focus:
- Identify why asymmetric traffic breaks a stateful design.

EP-08 — OSPF BETWEEN TUGGERS AND GUNNERS
Topics:
- OSPF neighbours, areas, costs, DR/BDR
- Route selection and summarisation
Story:
Static routes become fragile as services move between the two data centres.
Learning outcomes:
- Implement and reason about OSPF for enterprise traffic.
- Predict best-path changes after link failure.
- Distinguish neighbour formation problems from route-advertisement problems.
Optional mini-lab:
- Build a two-site OSPF design with Area 0.

EP-09 — THE ROUTE THAT LOOKED BETTER
Topics:
- BGP peering
- Best-path attributes
- Route maps, filtering, local preference, MED
- Basic convergence concepts
Story:
The Department gains a second upstream provider, but outbound and inbound paths do not match the design.
Learning outcomes:
- Explain how BGP policy influences path choice.
- Separate local decisions from remote-network influence.
- Troubleshoot received, accepted, selected, and installed routes.
Season incident:
- Fibre failure between Tuggers and Gunners.
- Samwise must explain HA, OSPF, BGP, and session consequences together.

ADAPTIVE SESSION RULE

At the start of each episode, run a fast diagnostic:
1. Ask Samwise to explain the purpose of the technology.
2. Ask one behaviour-prediction question.
3. Ask for one likely verification step.

Then choose:
- COMPRESS: Strong answers; move quickly to scenario and exam distinctions.
- NORMAL: Partial understanding; use the standard episode.
- REINFORCE: Weak mental model; narrow the episode and build the foundation.
- SKIP/REASSIGN: Demonstrated mastery; record evidence and move the time to a weak area.

Do not raise confidence because an episode was completed. Raise it only when the student demonstrates understanding.
