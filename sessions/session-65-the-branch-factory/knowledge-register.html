- HE LEARNS BY DERIVING, NOT RECEIVING. Every concept he built himself this session
  (drift, metadata variables, installation targets) is at ★★★★+. Every concept handed to him
  (header/footer packages, wizard names, the SD-WAN Manager trap) is at ★★☆☆☆ and one of them
  cost him a mark within the hour. STRONG IMPLICATION: convert more "given" material into
  small derivation questions, even trivia — a five-second question beats a stated fact.
- HE RESPONDS TO TENSION, NOT TOPICS. The best moments this session came from posed conflicts
  ("templates are shared but every site differs" → metadata variables). Frame material as a
  contradiction to resolve wherever possible.
- SUPPORTING CAST DOES REAL TEACHING WORK. Riley's "twins → cousins" and "drift with a naming
  convention" and "no travel budget" each completed a thought he had half-formed. Junior's
  preemptive confession made the review lesson land. USE THE CAST AS SCAFFOLDING — it is more
  effective than direct instruction with this student.
- HE PERFORMS BETTER UNDER NARRATIVE STAKES. Presenting to Penny produced his most complete,
  best-organised output of the session by a wide margin. CONSIDER MAKING THIS A RECURRING
  DEVICE: end sessions with a stakeholder he must brief.
- HE DOES NOT SELF-ENFORCE PROCESS. He self-enforces UNDERSTANDING. Any habit that must be
  maintained by discipline rather than curiosity will decay — build habits into the mechanics
  of the interaction (withheld marking) rather than into reminders.
- UNCERTAINTY SIGNALLING remains clean (11 sessions). He says when he doesn't know. Keep
  rewarding this with decode rather than hint-tax.
- ARTEFACT REQUESTS: three consecutive sessions of self-directed mid-session artefacts, then
