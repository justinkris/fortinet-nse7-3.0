Concepts Covered 13 concepts
  1. 01
    WHY BGP EXISTS (answering Junior). Link-state does not scale globally because every change must reach every router and every router must run SPF — and on a global network some link is always flapping. STUDENT'S OWN INSIGHT
    most of that computation produces NO CHANGE in the local forwarding decision. The work isn't merely expensive, it's wasted. From there: if the answer is the same regardless of Frankfurt's internals, you don't need Frankfurt's map — only reachability and direction.
  2. 02
    PATH VECTOR, formally positioned against the other two families
    RIP = how far (distance). OSPF = the whole map (link state). BGP = which way it came (path).
  3. 03
    AS_PATH LOOP PREVENTION — retrieved COLD and COMPLETE with the check condition attached ("if an AS sees its own AS in the AS_PATH it won't accept it"). This had been deferred four sessions; the S56/S57 registers recommended formal retirement as a retrieval target and fresh reteaching. It did NOT need reteaching. RETIRE THIS AS A CONCERN — it is owned. Emphasised the property that matters
    the check is LOCAL and INSTANT, needs no shared state, no timers, no hold-downs. That's precisely what you need when you have no map.
  4. 04
    THE IBGP/EBGP BEHAVIOURAL SPLIT — not a label, a behaviour change
  5. 05
    AS_PATH IS NOT PREPENDED WITHIN AN AS
    Therefore the loop check is structurally BLIND inside an AS. Inside AS 65001 BGP has no map, no hop count, no hold-down, and no working AS_PATH check — every loop-prevention tool is inoperative.
  6. 06
    IBGP SPLIT HORIZON (p.337) — the answer to (5). Formulated precisely and contrasted with the RIP version
    RIP = don't advertise back out the interface you learned it on (interface-scoped, directional). IBGP = a prefix learned from an IBGP peer is NEVER advertised to ANY other IBGP peer (peer-type-scoped). Key framing: this makes a loop STRUCTURALLY IMPOSSIBLE, not detected — a route can only take ONE IBGP hop ever, and you cannot build a circle from segments of length one.
  7. 07
    THE CRITICAL EXCEPTION the student needed handed to him
    split horizon says nothing about where the route ENTERED the AS. EBGP-learned routes are freely advertised to IBGP peers. The rule only bites on the SECOND internal hop. HQ→MEL is legal; MEL→BNE is not.
  8. 08
    FULL MESH REQUIREMENT as the forced consequence. n(n-1)/2. Student computed 28 for eight sites by deriving the summation 7+6+5+4+3+2+1 rather than reciting the formula. Operational framing (matters more than the number)
    adding the 9th site adds EIGHT sessions and requires touching EVERY existing router — a branch turnup becomes a fleet-wide change window. Study guide wording: "difficult to administer and not scalable."
  9. 09
    BGP NEXT-HOP RESOLUTION — the actual fault
    IBGP PRESERVES the NEXT_HOP unchanged; HQ does not insert itself. Melbourne was told to send to 172.20.0.1 (Javier's loopback, AS 65300) and had no route to it. A path whose next hop is unresolvable is VALID BUT NOT BEST — sits in the database forever, never installed, never withdrawn, NEVER LOGGED.
  10. 10
    STATUS CODE FORENSICS
    `*` = valid, `>` = best, `i` = internal. `*i` without `>` on a SINGLE-path prefix means it is not losing a competition — it is INELIGIBLE. The whole diagnosis lived in one missing character.
  11. 11
    next-hop-self
    applied on the BORDER router, toward IBGP peers, for EBGP-learned routes. Trade-off taught explicitly: kills traffic engineering and concentrates the failure domain on HQ. Correct for LoFinance ONLY because there is no direct MEL–SG circuit — "you are not losing a path you had." Would be wrong in a real multi-exit topology.
  12. 12
    ACCIDENTAL TRANSIT AS, now with live topology
    EBGP-learned routes are advertised to other EBGP peers by default. The moment London comes up, with zero misconfiguration, LoFinance backhauls SG↔LON. Defence is OUTBOUND route filtering (prefix-list/route-map), NOT AS_PATH. Student retrieved this correctly and cold from S54.
