Department of Fish · v2 — Supplementary Analogy Prompts
==============================================================================

One prompt per supplementary cheatsheet analogy illustration. Drop the
generated PNG into this folder using the exact filename listed below and the
placeholder inside the analogy callout will vanish.

STYLE (applies to every image):
  Flat cartoon illustration, detailed but visually simple. Colourful using a
  light, gentle palette — soft pastels and light saturated tones (pale sky
  blue, mint, peach, buttercream, dusty rose, sage, lavender, warm ochre)
  chosen to suit the scene rather than a fixed theme. No dominant single-
  colour wash; use several light colours together to keep the image cheerful
  without being loud. Clean thin dark outlines, flat solid fills, minimal or
  no shading. No gradients, no cross-hatching, no photorealism, no painterly
  texture, no neon, no heavy saturation. Small charming details are welcome;
  noise and grit are not. Characters, key props and one clear focal action
  should be recognisable at small size. 16:9 landscape composition. Slight
  government-office comedy tone, but the technical elements should still be
  recognisable. No vendor logos, no readable brand names on hardware.

SUPP-01 — A template is an author, not a notebook
------------------------------------------------------------
Save as: supp-01-analogy-template-as-author.png

Underlying analogy:
  A template is a person writing in notebooks — not a notebook itself.
    • Each managed FortiGate has its own notebook (device database).
    • The template is an author who writes the same passage into thirty
      notebooks at once.
    • If someone has already scribbled by hand in a notebook and the author
      writes over that page — the author wins. Every install.
    • Nothing leaves the notebook until someone posts it. That posting is
      the install.

Scene:
  A tall, calm cartoon figure — "The Template" — stands at the centre,
  drawn as a friendly author holding a fountain pen. In front of them, a
  long line of small identical notebooks fans out across a wooden table.
  The author reaches out with impossibly long arms (or many arms, in a
  gentle wraparound gesture) and writes the SAME sentence into every open
  notebook simultaneously. Each notebook has a small tag labelled with a
  device name (FGT-01, FGT-02, FGT-03, …). Off to one side, a tiny
  hand-holding-a-pen has been scribbling in one of the notebooks by hand —
  and the author's pen is currently crossing right over those scribbles,
  overwriting them. A separate small figure at the far edge of the table
  is dropping a completed notebook into a "POST" box — this represents the
  install. Optional: a soft aquarium wall glow in the background to place
  the scene at ZOO HQ.

Focal action:
  The author writing the same passage into many notebooks at once, with
  one notebook visibly being overwritten on top of prior hand-scribbles.

    4. INSTALL — which install operation moves it forward
