The Four Boxes on Coach's Whiteboard

One rainy Monday at Halloran's Gym. One empty whiteboard. One rookie who learns that nobody gets stopped by a list.

They don't tap out to information — they tap out to enforcement.

The Security Fabric information chain, told the way it happened: learn → share → consume → enforce.

The receiver holds the gate

Coach MacThat's the word. FortiGate asks, FortiAnalyzer decides. The receiver holds the gate, not the sender.

One direction is reporting. Two directions is a Fabric.
RoccoFortiGate gives logs to FAZ. That arrow I know.
Coach MacNow defend the second one. The FortiGate ends up doing something it couldn't do before — enforcing on something it never learned by itself.

Something has to come back down. Coach writes FortiMQ in box two: the messaging layer between Fabric members. Not the intelligence. Not the object. The pipe.

Doc SilverConfigured correctly, you see nothing. Configured wrong, everything downstream looks like a different problem. That's the fifty points people lose without knowing they lost it.

Mental note
FortiMQ is the transport. When the pipe breaks, the failure shows up somewhere else, wearing a disguise.

Act VI The Trap — Match Is Not Action
Panel 08 — The Trap images/p08-the-trap.png Rocco confidently crossing off the last box on the whiteboard while, behind him, Doc holds up her notebook showing a log line — and Mick appears in the doorway wrapping a wrist.
Comic book panel, bold inks, halftone, tense composition. The young fighter, cocky, reaches up and draws an X through the FOURTH box on the whiteboard chain. Behind him the cornerwoman holds up her open spiral notebook toward the reader showing a single stark highlighted line (unreadable scribble representing a log entry). In the doorway a huge veteran fighter leans against the frame wrapping his wrist with tape, watching with a flat knowing expression. Dramatic irony staging — the reader can see what Rocco can't. The log exists. It names your policy. And you just crossed off the wrong box.

Break-it scenario: everything configured, host reaches a destination that's definitely on the feed — traffic goes straight through. The log reads: connection allowed, policy named. Rocco crosses off Authorize... and Enforce.

Coach MacYou crossed off the wrong one, Rocc. Read what the log actually said. The traffic reached the rule you wanted. That's the rule matching. Nobody said the rule did anything.
Big MickRight door. Wrong doorman.
The UFC trap that cost the round: "allowed, policy X" proves the traffic found the right rule and nothing happened to it. Match is not action. Fortinet scores building and proving separately — on purpose.
🧠
Mental note
A log line proves two things separately: the traffic reached the right rule (match), and something was done about it (action). Demand both.

Half the board eliminated in thirty seconds
RoccoCheck the name on the sheet before the doorman.
Coach MacRight call — it's the cheap one.
Name's not on the sheet → the doorman never had a chance. Problem is upstream: indicator, pipe, or feed never populated.
Name's on the sheet → doorman problem. Intelligence arrived and the policy still let it walk: profile or action.

One look. Two categories separated. That's how you spend thirty seconds instead of ten minutes when the clock is real.

🧠
Mental note
When enforcement fails, check the consumable object first — it's the cheap check that splits upstream from downstream.
Act VIII Doc's Rule — Green Is a Light, Not Evidence

"Configured is intention. Verified is truth."
RoccoEverything's configured... testing. We still need testing.
Doc SilverShow me the log. A judge doesn't score "I tested it". A judge scores what you put in front of them.

And the final challenge lands the same lesson from another angle: the dashboard shows the device connected, logs arriving, everything green. Proof of box one?

Coach MacConnected is a transport state, Rocc. It isn't a trust state.
Doc SilverGreen is a light. It isn't evidence.
Twice in one round, two directions: a status indicator shows a condition, not an outcome — same family of error as match vs action. (Exact FAZ device states: requires official Fortinet source verification — that's UFC-02.)
