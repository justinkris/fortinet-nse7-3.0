The Four Empty Boxes

ACT 03 · BOX TWO · 50 PTS

The Pipe Is Not The Payload

Logs flow up — that's the direction everybody knows. Coach drew a second arrow coming back down and said: "defend this one." Rocco mashed three things into one sentence, so Coach split them apart: the pipe, the thing travelling down the pipe, and the object the pipe fills up.

🧠
MENTAL NOTE
Three separate things: the transport (FortiMQ), the intelligence travelling through it, and the consumable object it fills. Collapse them and you'll troubleshoot the wrong box.
ACT 04 · BOX THREE · THE TURNING POINT · 50 + 50 PTS

The Name and the Sheet

This is where the session stalled. Indicator vs Fabric Feed — Rocco couldn't split them while they wore Fortinet vocabulary. So Coach put his coffee cup on the table and took the whole thing to the pub where Mick works the door three nights a week.

4.1
ufc01-p08-pub-splash.png
Comic book SPLASH PAGE, dramatic wide shot inside a warm old pub at night. LEFT: a huge doorman (Mick, 42, military bearing, folded arms) fills the doorway, rain outside behind him. RIGHT: behind the bar, a paper sheet pinned to the wall titled "BARRED" with a list of names, one name circled in red. A dotted sight-line connects the doorman's eyes to the sheet. In a corner inset, a shifty patron in a flat cap approaches the door. Two floating label boxes: "THE NAME = one piece of intelligence" pointing at the circled name, and "THE SHEET = the list the door policy points at" pointing at the paper. Rich amber pub lighting, bold noir inking.
Coach: "Somebody gets barred. One name. One face. That's one piece of intelligence about one bad actor. Mick doesn't memorise faces — there's a sheet behind the bar. Names go on, names come off. Mick's instruction never changes: anyone on that sheet doesn't come in. The sheet changes underneath him."
CoachTwo different things there, Rocc. The barred name. And the sheet. Which is the intelligence, which does the door policy point at?
RoccoThe intelligence is the name… the list the door points at is the sheet.
CoachThat's it. Now put the labels back on.

RIGHT half: a clipboard sheet with many entries labelled "FABRIC FEED — the sheet — the container a policy points at; contents change, policy never edited". CoachCareful — it's a list that something else fills. That something else is box two.
Fifty points each. People mix them up under the clock because both sound like "the bad thing".
The trap Rocco hit: the concepts weren't the problem — the vocabulary was. Stripped of Fortinet words, he solved it instantly. On fight night, translate the question into plain terms first, then put the labels back on.
🧠
MENTAL NOTE
Indicator = the name. Fabric Feed = the sheet. You enforce on the sheet, never on the name — and the policy never gets edited when the sheet changes.

ACT 07 · BREAK IT · THE TRAP

Right Door. Wrong Doorman.

Coach broke the chain: everything configured, a destination definitely on the feed — and the traffic sails straight through. The log exists, and it names Rocco's policy. Rocco crossed off Authorize… and then crossed off Enforce. The second one cost him.

Comic book style decision panel split diagonally. TOP-LEFT: a hand lifts the paper sheet behind the bar — cheap, one look. Two outcome captions branch from it like a flowchart: "NAME NOT ON SHEET → problem is UPSTREAM (indicator / pipe / feed)" and "NAME ON SHEET → problem is the DOORMAN (profile / action)". Common UFC trap (Rocco's actual mistake): clearing the enforcement box the moment your policy name appears in the log. "Allowed, policy X" means the traffic found the right rule and nothing happened to it. That single confusion cost two suspects on the troubleshoot.
🧠
MENTAL NOTE
When enforcement fails, check the sheet first — it's the one cheap look that splits upstream (feed never populated) from downstream (policy not acting).

The Distinctions on the Wall

CONFIGURED ≠ WORKING
Everything on the board is intention until traffic proves it. Doc's blank page is the whole lesson.
MATCH ≠ ACTION
"Allowed, policy X" proves the traffic found the rule. It says nothing about what the rule did.
INDICATOR ≠ FEED
The name is intelligence about one entity. The sheet is the container a policy points at. Enforce on the sheet.
PIPE ≠ PAYLOAD
FortiMQ is transport. When the pipe is wrong, the failure surfaces somewhere else and wears a different disguise.
STATUS ≠ VERIFICATION
Connected is a transport state, not a trust state. Green is a light — it isn't evidence.
CLASSIFICATION ≠ ENFORCEMENT
A populated feed stops nothing. Nobody gets stopped by a list — they get stopped by the door.
